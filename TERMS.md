## 1. What Qhaphela is

Qhaphela is a free, open-source tool that reads job advertisements you are
already viewing and estimates how likely they are to be fraudulent. It explains
its reasoning, warns about requests that could enable identity theft, and helps
you compare your CV against what a posting asks for.

It exists to protect South African job seekers - especially young people and
first-time job seekers - from recruitment fraud.

---

## 2. The most important thing on this page

> ### Qhaphela gives you an opinion, not a verdict.
>
> A **low** score does not mean a job is safe.
> A **high** score does not mean a company is dishonest.
>
> **Always verify an employer yourself before sharing anything personal.**

Qhaphela is a decision-support tool. The final judgement is yours, and it should
be, because you have context the software does not.

---

## 3. What Qhaphela cannot do

Being clear about the limits is part of using it safely.

- **It reads words, not intentions.** A careful scammer who avoids obvious
  phrasing can score low. Fraud often begins *after* you apply, by email or
  WhatsApp, where Qhaphela cannot see.
- **It does not verify companies.** No company registry, CIPC record or
  LinkedIn profile is checked. The "contact and domain checks" describe what
  appears in the posting text - nothing more.
- **It can be wrong in both directions.** Small and informal employers can look
  unusual to software. Qhaphela is deliberately built not to penalise informal
  wording, but no automated system is perfect.
- **Its model was trained on synthetic data.** Real-world evaluation is
  reported openly, on a small sample, and stated as such. It is not a certified
  accuracy figure.
- **It is not legal, financial or career advice.**

## 4. Using it responsibly

You agree to use Qhaphela to protect yourself and others, and **not** to:

- treat its output as proof that a company or person is fraudulent
- publish or repeat an accusation against a real employer based on a score alone
- use it to harass, defame or target any person or organisation
- attempt to overload or attack the scoring service or any job platform

Employers named in postings have not been assessed as businesses. A high score
reflects **wording in an advertisement**, which may not even have been written
by the company it names - impersonation is one of the frauds Qhaphela detects.

---

## 5. Reporting

You may report suspicious postings. Reports are stored **on your own computer**
and are not published or shared. Report honestly: submit only postings you
genuinely believe are suspicious.

---

## 6. Your CV and personal information

Uploading a CV is optional. It is processed on your own machine and never
stored (see the [Privacy Policy](PRIVACY.md)).

**Never send your ID number, passport, bank details or any payment to an
employer you have not independently verified.** No legitimate South African
employer requires these to consider an application.
